Grid EA Settings Audit & Stress Test

Grid, martingale and averaging systems do not fail because of a bad setting. They fail because nobody checked what the settings add up to when the market moves one way for long enough. This worksheet does that arithmetic before the account does it for you.

A smooth equity curve is not evidence of safety. Grid systems show small, regular gains for long periods and then a single large loss that removes the account. Leveraged trading of this kind can lose money rapidly; nothing here predicts a result, and no setting makes a grid system safe.

3 — Margin and stop-out: the number that decides the ending

Whether a grid survives to its final level is not decided by the strategy. It is decided by leverage, because leverage sets how much margin each additional level consumes and therefore how much equity is left when the last level opens.

Margin required per lot = (Contract size x Price) / Leverage
Margin level % = (Equity / Used margin) x 100
Stop-out occurs when Margin level falls to the broker's threshold, commonly 50%

Worked comparison — EUR/USD at 1.10, $1,000 account

Using a 1.13-lot cumulative position, which is roughly what a 0.01 start reaches by its tenth level on a 30-pip step with a 1.5 multiplier.

This is the counter-intuitive part. Higher leverage does not make a grid safer by giving it room. It lets the system keep adding levels until the loss is large enough to be unrecoverable. Lower leverage stops it earlier and caps the damage. If your broker offers a choice, the lower setting is the protective one — and a grid EA marketed as needing high leverage is telling you something about its risk.
