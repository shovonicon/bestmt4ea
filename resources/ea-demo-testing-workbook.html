EA Demo Testing Workbook: A Four-Week Test You Can Actually Judge

Most EA demo tests prove nothing because they have no plan: the robot runs until the balance looks good or bad, and the trader decides by mood. This workbook fixes the test in advance — settings, duration, loss limit, and the scorecard you will judge it by — so the result means something either way.

3. The scorecard

  • Expectancy: average win × win rate, minus average loss × loss rate. Negative expectancy on demo does not turn positive live.
  • Profit factor (gross wins ÷ gross losses): below 1.1 is noise, not an edge.
  • Largest losing streak: count it, then ask whether you would have kept the EA running through it with real money.
  • Slippage and spread reality: did live-session spreads match what the strategy assumes?
  • Behaviour score: did it ever hold a loser far beyond its stated logic, add to a losing position in a way the sales page didn't mention, or trade through a news spike it claims to avoid?
The workbook rule: judge the test you planned, not the test you got. Changing settings mid-test restarts the four weeks — or the result is a story, not a measurement.
