EA Settings Record Sheet: Change One Thing, Write It Down

An expert advisor has dozens of inputs, and every undocumented change makes the next result unreadable: you no longer know which settings produced which trades. This sheet is the record to keep for every EA — what ran, when it changed, and what happened next.

3. Rules that keep the record honest

  • One change at a time. Two changes share the credit and the blame, and you learn neither.
  • Export and save the .set file after every accepted change, named with the date. Your future self cannot reconstruct settings from memory.
  • Magic numbers: one unique number per EA per pair. Shared magics make two robots manage each other's trades.
  • Record the changes you rejected too, with the reason. A rejected change tried again in three months wastes the same three months.
  • Vendor update? Save the old .set, note the version change here, and re-run at least a short demo before the new build touches live money.
The settings rule: if it isn't written on this sheet, it didn't happen — and any result you cannot trace to a settings record is a result you cannot repeat.
