Divergence Trading Settings Worksheet

Divergence — price making a new extreme while an oscillator does not — is a timing clue, not a signal by itself. This worksheet sets out honest settings by timeframe and the confirmation rules that separate tradable divergence from chart decoration.

1. The honesty box (read first)

  • Every divergence indicator needs confirmed swing points, and swings are only confirmed after the fact. A "non-repainting" divergence tool therefore signals late by construction — typically by the pivot confirmation bars — or it repaints. There is no third option.
  • Divergence against a strong trend can persist for a very long time ("the oscillator can diverge longer than your account can stay solvent"). Trade divergence with the trend (hidden divergence) for continuation, and against it only with a separate confirmation.

3. Confirmation rules to choose (pick before testing)

  • Structure confirmation: wait for a break of the local trendline or the most recent minor swing in your direction.
  • Candle confirmation: an engulfing or pin bar at the divergence extreme on the trading timeframe.
  • Momentum reset: oscillator returns toward mid-range before entry, proving the move is not simply continuing.
  • Invalidation: a close beyond the price extreme that created the divergence. If price makes a further extreme with the oscillator now agreeing, the idea is over.

4. The live test

  • Screenshot every signal at the moment it appears and again 10 bars later. Compare: did the signal move or vanish? (If yes, it repaints, whatever the description said.)
  • Log 30 signals with your chosen confirmation rule applied and not applied. The difference in results is what the confirmation rule is worth.
The worksheet rule: divergence tells you momentum is tiring. It never tells you price must turn. Your confirmation rule and your invalidation are the trade; divergence is just the invitation to look.
