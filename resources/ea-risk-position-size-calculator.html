EA Risk & Position Size Calculator

An expert advisor can open positions without asking you. That is the point of it, and that is the danger. This worksheet works out, in advance, the largest position you are willing to have open — so the EA's own risk setting confirms your decision instead of replacing it.

Leveraged trading can lose money faster than you can react. Most retail accounts that trade leveraged products lose money. Nothing on this sheet predicts a result or guarantees a profit — it exists to make the loss you are accepting a number you chose rather than a surprise.

Step 1 — Decide the money, not the lots

Choose the amount you are prepared to lose on a single trade if the stop is hit. Traders who start from lot size and work backwards almost always risk more than they intended, because the stop distance is decided last and then stretched to fit.

Risk per trade (money) = Account balance x Risk %
Lots = Risk per trade / (Stop distance in pips x Pip value per lot)

Worked example — EUR/USD

1

Account balance $10,000. Risk chosen: 1%.

2

Risk per trade = 10,000 x 0.01 = $100.

3

The EA's stop sits 20 pips from entry. EUR/USD pip value is $10 per standard lot.

4

Lots = 100 / (20 x 10) = 0.50 lots.

5

Result: if the stop is hit, roughly $100 leaves the account. That is the whole point of the calculation.

Worked example — USD/JPY, where the pip value is not $10

1

Account balance $5,000. Risk chosen: 0.5% = $25.

2

USD/JPY at 150.00: pip value = (0.01 / 150.00) x 100,000 = $6.67 per lot.

3

Stop distance 25 pips. Lots = 25 / (25 x 6.67) = 0.15 lots.

Using $10 here instead of $6.67 would have sized the position 50% too large — a mistake that stays invisible until the stop is hit.

When neither is USD you need a further conversion, which is most of the reason indices, crypto CFDs and exotic crosses are best sized in the platform's own risk-per-trade field rather than by hand.

Read the 2% column and then count how many consecutive losses it takes to lose a fifth of the account: ten. A strategy with a 40% win rate will produce runs of eight to ten losses over a long enough sample. That is the arithmetic behind the usual advice to stay at or below 1%.

Why an EA needs a hard cap as well as a percentage

  • A percentage risk setting assumes each trade stands alone. A grid or averaging EA does not trade that way — each level adds to a single losing position, so the "risk per trade" is really a slice of the whole basket.
  • Set a maximum number of open positions as well as a risk percentage, and decide the equity figure at which you will intervene by hand.
  • If the EA lets you cap total drawdown, set it below the level that would make you act emotionally. A limit you will not respect is worse than no limit, because it hides the risk you are actually carrying.
