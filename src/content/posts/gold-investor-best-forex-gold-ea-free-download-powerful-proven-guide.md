## The problem: "free" answers the wrong question

When you search for a gold EA free download, you are asking a price question, but the question that decides your account is a risk question. Free and paid expert advisors fail in exactly the same ways: oversized positions, stops placed inside gold's noise, strategies that add to losers, and results that were tuned to a broker that is not yours. The price tag does not change any of that.

What the price tag does change is your leverage over the information. With a paid product you at least have a vendor with a reputation to protect and, sometimes, a licence and a refund policy. With a free download you often have neither, which means the burden of verification moves entirely to you. That is not a reason to avoid free EAs — many are excellent and open source — but it is a reason to check before you install, every time.

The specific danger on gold is that the file you run has your account number and your broker connection, and an expert advisor can open, modify and close positions. A compiled `.ex4` or `.ex5` gives you no way to read what it does. Repackaged robots with added routines are a known and recurring problem in the free-download space, which is why the platforms themselves warn users about the source of what they run. On a currency pair that risk is already serious. On **XAUUSD**, where a single trade can move far enough to matter, it is more so.

Then there is the quieter problem: almost no free gold EA publishes the number that decides whether you keep the account, which is the maximum drawdown on equity over a stated sample. You get a win rate and a rising curve. You do not get the worst week. And the worst week is where free, paid, and everything else are actually decided. A free gold EA is not safe or unsafe in the abstract; it is a match or a mismatch with your broker, your settings and your hours. ## How do you verify a free gold EA download before installing it?

Four checks, in this order, and each takes less than a minute. If any one fails, do not install.

| Check | Pass | Fail | Why it matters on gold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Publisher | A named author or organisation with a history | No name, a throwaway handle | You cannot ask about updates or verify claims |
| Licence | A stated licence (MIT, GPL-3.0, Apache-2.0) with a link | "Free" and nothing else | No licence means no clarity and no update path |
| Source | Readable `.mq4`/`.mq5` or a public repository | A compiled binary only | You must be able to check for grid and martingale logic |
| Drawdown | A stated maximum **equity** drawdown over a stated trade count | A win rate and a rising curve only | Gold's bad weeks are deep; the downside decides survival |

Two of those are evidence of identity and two are evidence of behaviour, and you need both halves. A robot with great drawdown numbers and no publisher is a well-drawn chart. A robot with a famous publisher and no published drawdown is a brand asking for trust it has not earned on this instrument.

When you check the source, you do not need to read MQL. Search for the words that describe dangerous behaviour: the order function that opens a position, and any lot multiplier, grid step or averaging routine that adds to losers. Confirm that a stop-loss is set on every position. If the file is compiled and offers no source, you cannot run those searches, and that inability is a property of the download rather than of your skill.

## How do you set up a gold EA on MT4 or MT5?

Install it the same way for either platform, with one difference that trips up a lot of people: the files are not interchangeable. **MetaTrader 4** runs **MQL4** experts — `.mq4` source and `.ex4` compiled. **MetaTrader 5** runs **MQL5** experts — `.mq5` source and `.ex5` compiled. A gold robot written for one will not simply run on the other, and a download page that does not say which platform its file targets has failed its most basic job.

| Step | MetaTrader 4 | MetaTrader 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Find the data folder | File, then Open Data Folder | File, then Open Data Folder |
| Copy the expert into | `MQL4/Experts` | `MQL5/Experts` |
| Compile | MetaEditor, F7, or right-click and Modify | MetaEditor, F7 |
| Attach | Drag onto a XAUUSD chart | Drag onto a XAUUSD chart |
| Enable | AutoTrading button in the toolbar | Algo Trading button in the toolbar |
| Verify | Experts tab in the Terminal shows initialisation | Experts tab in the Toolbox shows initialisation |

Once it is attached, set the four inputs that matter before you touch anything else: risk per trade, stop distance, maximum spread and session hours. On gold, a sensible starting shape is 0.25 to 0.5 percent risk per trade, a stop of 2.00 to 3.00 dollars of price on an M5 or higher timeframe, a maximum spread around 20 to 30 points, and trading only during the London to New York overlap. Confirm that every value saved after you press OK, because a setting that silently reverts is worse than one you never changed.

One gold-specific practical point: the symbol name is not always `XAUUSD`. Some brokers quote `XAUUSD.m`, `GOLD`, or a suffix of their own, and an expert that references the wrong name will simply not trade. Check Market Watch for the exact spelling, and treat a missing initialisation line in the Experts tab as a symbol or history problem rather than a broken download.

## Which settings actually lower the risk on gold?

Risk per trade does the heavy lifting, because it converts a strategy's losing streaks into account drawdown. Everything else refines the result. Set the risk input first, and let the lot size follow from the stop.

| Setting | Beginner starting value | What it controls | What goes wrong if it is wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Risk per trade | 0.25 to 0.5 percent | How much a losing streak costs you | At 2 percent, five losses in a row is a 10 percent drawdown |
| Stop distance | 2.00 to 3.00 dollars (M5 or higher) | How much room gold needs | Too tight, the stop is inside the noise; too wide, the lot shrinks |
| Maximum spread | 20 to 30 points, or what you actually see | Which trades are allowed | Set to zero it disables the filter and trades the worst windows |
| Session hours | London open to New York overlap | When the EA may trade | Thin hours combine a wide spread with a small range |
| Daily loss limit | 2 percent of equity | Total loss in a day | Without it, a bad day compounds into a bad week |

The reason risk comes first is that it is the only input that maps directly onto your survival. A strategy decides how many trades you lose in a row; your risk per trade decides what that run costs. Same EA, same signals, 0.5 percent versus 2 percent risk, is a fourfold difference in how deep the same losing streak cuts. That is why professionals talk about position sizing before they talk about entries — and why the calculator tools on this site keep reappearing on pages about gold EAs.

A spread cap is worth calling out because it is the most commonly disabled input. Setting the maximum spread to zero does not widen your tolerance; it switches the filter off, so the EA trades through the exact thin-book windows it should avoid. A blocked trade you had no edge on costs nothing. A trade filled through a 100-point spread on a 200-point target is already behind when it opens.

## How do you test a free gold EA before going live?

Test it in four stages, and do not skip ahead. Each stage exists to catch a specific failure that the previous one cannot.

1. **Read the source.** Confirm the order function and the stop logic, and look for any grid step or lot multiplier. This catches dangerous strategy design before you spend time testing it.
2. **Backtest on real ticks.** Run the Strategy Tester on your broker's XAUUSD with real ticks where available. Read the modelling quality figure and remember it describes how faithfully price was reconstructed, not whether the strategy has an edge.
3. **Forward-test on demo for four weeks.** Collect at least thirty closed trades, including one high-impact event. Compare the demo record against the backtest on the same dates, because this is where spread, slippage and swap stop being assumptions.
4. **Size a live test at half your intended risk.** Only if the demo matches the backtest, go live at reduced risk until the live results confirm the demo. Increase only after the records agree again.

The most common reason a free gold EA "stops working" after being promising on demo is not a change in the market. It is a change in conditions: a different broker, a different session, or a lot size that grew because the balance did. Test the exact configuration you intend to run, on the feed you intend to run it on, and re-check it whenever any of those change.

## How do you tell a broker mismatch from a broken EA?

Before you blame the robot, rule out the three conditions that change between accounts and never appear in a review.

**Spread.** Compare the average spread on your feed with the one quoted in the EA's documentation. On gold this is not a rounding difference. A scalper targeting eight dollars per trade cannot survive a three-dollar spread that widens to nine during a data release, and no setting fixes a cost that exceeds the target.

**Execution.** Log the difference between the price you asked for and the price you got. Twenty trades is enough to see a pattern. When slippage on entry is consistently against you, the strategy is taxed before it starts, and the same file on a lower-latency server can behave differently.

**Symbol and contract size.** Confirm your broker's gold symbol matches the one the EA expects, suffix included, and confirm the contract size and digit count. A two-decimal gold feed and a three-decimal one do not produce the same lot from the same risk percentage, so the risk you set is not always the risk you get.

### What a fair comparison looks like

Run the same version, the same preset and the same dates on both accounts, then compare four figures instead of one: average spread paid, average slippage, the worst single-day equity fall, and the number of trades taken. If the trade count differs materially, the two runs are not testing the same thing and the comparison is void.

When the counts match and the costs are higher, the EA is fine and the broker is the variable. When the counts match, the costs match, and the curve still diverges, look at the logic. That sequence is the only reliable way to separate a mismatch from a genuine defect, and it costs a fortnight rather than an argument.

### Two more conditions people forget

Swap and commission are charged per position and per lot, so a system that holds overnight pays for the privilege. On gold, carry can be a meaningful fraction of a small target, and it does not appear anywhere on a marketing page. Read the contract specification, not the headline spread.

Then there is drift. A preset tuned on one volatility regime behaves differently in a quieter or wilder market, and that is not a fault in the file. Re-test after any material change to the feed, the spread or the session, and treat the old result as history rather than a promise.

Keep the comparison on one page: four columns, two accounts, the same dates, and a line recording the decision you drew from it. When the same question returns in six months, with a different robot and the same broker, that page is what stops you repeating the experiment from memory. Most traders do not lack information about gold. They lack a record of what they already tested, so the same month of tuition gets paid twice.

The point is not to defend free expert advisors. It is to stop yourself from replacing a system that works on the wrong feed with one that will fail on it, and to stop yourself keeping one that is simply badly built because the demo looked good once.
