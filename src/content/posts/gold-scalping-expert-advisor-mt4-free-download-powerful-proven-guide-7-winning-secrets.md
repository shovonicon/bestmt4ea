Type "gold scalping expert advisor MT4 free download" into any search box and you get a wall of files. Most are free. A few even work. Almost none of them explain the part that actually decides whether you keep trading next month.

Here is the uncomfortable truth about scalping gold (XAUUSD). The entry signal is the least important part of the system. A good entry fired through a 45-point spread, with a stop sitting inside the noise and three positions open at once, loses money with total consistency. A mediocre entry fired through a tight spread, with a wide stop and ATR-sized lots, survives long enough to improve.

That gap — between what a download promises and what your account actually needs — is where most people lose. And it is where this guide lives.

Notice what the marketing leaves out. It sells a strategy and hides the operating conditions. It rarely mentions the spread you will actually pay at 14:30 on a news day, or that a 15-point stop is close to a coin flip against gold's ordinary five-minute range. Those details are not secrets either. They are simply unglamorous, and unglamorous does not sell a download.

The seven factors below are those operating conditions. Enforce them and a free EA can behave like a serious tool. Ignore them and even a well-built one will slowly bleed an account while you blame the logic.

## The problem is not the robot, it is the seven things around it

You have probably done this already. You downloaded a free gold scalper, attached it to an XAUUSD chart, watched it take a few trades, and felt that small flicker of certainty. Then a week passed. The equity curve went sideways and down. You wondered whether the EA was broken, or whether you picked a bad one.

It was probably neither. It was probably the environment you ran it in.

Gold is not EURUSD with a different label. Its spreads move. Its volatility arrives in bursts. It reacts to US data, to the dollar, to real yields, and to headlines that have nothing to do with technical analysis. A scalping robot designed on a stable pair gets handed a market that changes character three times a day. If the rules around it are loose, the results look random — and random results get blamed on the tool.

**This is the part nobody selling you an EA wants to say out loud.** The download is the easy bit. What decides the outcome is a short list of boring decisions: what spread you accept, which hours you trade, where your stop goes, how big the position is, how many positions run at once, when you stop for the day, and when you re-check the whole thing.

## The story of a two-thousand-dollar account, in nine days

Tomas emailed in March. He had a 2,000 USD account, an MT4 gold scalper he downloaded for free, and a plan he described as "small stops, quick exits".

His stop was 15 points. That is 0.15 USD on gold. The London session was calm that first week, and he finished it up 180 USD. He felt good. He increased his lot size, because the account was clearly working.

Then the second Tuesday arrived. An inflation print landed at 13:30 UTC. Gold jumped, then reversed, then jumped again. His 15-point stop was inside the first candle. Tomas got stopped, re-entered, got stopped again, and re-entered a third time, now with a bigger lot because he wanted the money back. He also had a second and third position open by then, all long, all gold, all effectively the same bet.

By Friday his account showed 610 USD. Nine trading days, 1,390 USD gone, and not one of those losses came from a bad signal. Every one of them came from a rule he had never written down.

**The cost of doing nothing is not abstract.** It is the difference between a trader who is still in the market in six months with a funded method, and a trader who has decided that "gold scalping does not work" and moved on to a new download that will treat him the same way.

## None of these are secrets, and all of them are ignored

The seven factors below are not hidden knowledge. Every experienced gold trader knows them. Almost none of the people running a free EA enforce them, because enforcing them takes discipline, and a robot cannot supply discipline you have not programmed into it or surrounded it with.

Read them as a survival checklist. A gold scalping expert advisor does not survive on clever logic. It survives because the person running it built a box the robot cannot escape — and the seven walls of that box are spread, session, stop, size, position count, daily loss limit, and a re-test date.

## 1. Your spread budget decides more than your entry does

Your first number in gold scalping is not the entry price — it is the maximum spread you will pay. Write it down in points and refuse to trade above it, because on XAUUSD the spread can double in the same minute a signal fires.

On gold, one point is 0.01 USD, and one full lot is 100 ounces. That makes the arithmetic unusually easy: 1 point on a 1.00 lot costs about 1 USD. So 20 points of spread costs roughly 20 USD every round trip on a full lot, before the market has moved at all. Spread is not a rounding error here; it is a standing tax.

Do the monthly maths once and you will never treat it as noise again. Trade twice a day, twenty days a month, and pay 20 points per trade on a 0.05 lot: that is about 40 USD a month in pure friction. Ran on a full lot, it is 800 USD a month. Your strategy has to beat that number before it earns anything.

Spread also does not sit still. It widens into every high-impact release, and it explodes through the rollover window, when the broker's liquidity providers step away and the quote thins out. The same 0.05 lot that costs around 15 USD to open at 14:00 can cost 90 USD at 22:00 for no reason other than the hour. That is why the session rule in the next section is not a lifestyle preference — it is a cost-control decision.

Commissions and swap sit on top of that. A raw-spread account with a per-lot commission is often cheaper overall than a wider-spread account, but only if your volume makes the fee worth it, and only if the raw spread really is raw. Swap is a smaller factor on positions held for minutes, but it is not zero once you start leaving trades open through the evening.

Keep a spread column in your log. If your average paid spread creeps up, your edge is shrinking even when your win rate looks the same. And remember that no ceiling removes the risk of loss; it only removes one avoidable one.

## 2. Which session should a gold scalper actually trade?

Trade one window — the London–New York overlap — and leave the thin hours alone. That is where XAUUSD spreads are usually tightest and volume is deepest, which is the environment a scalper was designed for.

The table below shows indicative spread behaviour by session. Treat it as a shape, not a promise: your own broker's numbers are the ones that count, so check them on a demo account first.

| Session (UTC) | Typical XAUUSD spread | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Asian, 00:00–07:00 | 30–60 points | Thin, range-bound, widest spreads |
| London, 07:00–13:00 | 15–30 points | Strong directional moves, news spikes |
| London–New York overlap, 13:00–17:00 | 10–20 points | Tightest spread, deepest volume |
| New York late, 17:00–21:00 | 20–35 points | Liquidity fading, choppier |
| Rollover window, ~21:00–23:00 | 50–200+ points | Avoid entirely |

The Asian session is where most automated gold scalpers quietly lose. Volume is thin, so the same order size moves price further, and a small pullback looks like a trend because there is no depth to absorb it. Combined with a wider spread, every trade starts further behind. A robot with no time filter will happily take those trades all night and report a flat, slowly decaying equity curve.

There is also a daylight-saving trap. London and New York shift by an hour twice a year, so a hard-coded session window drifts out of alignment if you set it once and forget it. Re-check the window after every clock change, or use a filter that reads the server time rather than a fixed number of hours.

A scalper that runs around the clock is paying the worst spreads in the quiet hours for the privilege of taking the lowest-quality trades. Restrict your EA to one window in its inputs if it supports a time filter, or switch AutoTrading off outside that window by hand. ## 3. Where does a stop belong so gold's noise cannot reach it?

A gold stop belongs beyond the range price normally travels against you — past the recent swing or about 1.5 to 2 times ATR — not at a round number and not at a fixed point count. If your stop sits inside the noise, you are not being stopped out by being wrong; you are being stopped out by gold breathing.

XAUUSD can cover 150 to 300 points in a single busy five-minute window. A 15-point stop is not a tight stop; it is a random number generator that pays the broker. The old habit of counting pips the way you would on EURUSD simply does not transfer to gold.

Two practical placements work well:

- **Beyond the last swing.** Put the stop a buffer past the most recent swing high or low that would invalidate your idea. Add 20–30 points so a wick does not clip you.
- **From ATR.** Measure ATR(14) on your trading timeframe and multiply by 1.5 or 2.0. This adapts automatically when gold gets quiet or violent.

Stop orders also fill differently from what a backtest implies. A stop becomes a market order once it triggers, so in fast conditions you get filled at the next available price, not the one you set. Gold's gaps and spikes make this worse than it is on a major currency pair. Assume your real stop distance is a little wider than the label, and size as if it is.

Fixed point counts inherited from forex are the root of the problem. Fifty points on EURUSD and fifty points on gold are not comparable risks, because gold's range is simply larger. When you switch symbols, you have to switch the way you think about distance as well.

Round numbers attract resting orders, so avoid sitting your stop right on 2,000.00 or 2,050.00. A wider stop is not a riskier stop — it is a riskier stop only if you keep the same lot size, which is the mistake the next rule fixes. ## 4. How do you size a gold position from ATR instead of from feeling?

Size the position from the stop distance and your account risk, never from confidence. The formula is short: **lots = (account equity x risk percent) / (stop distance in points x value per point)**. Everything you need is already on the chart.

Work it through once with real numbers. You have a 5,000 USD account and you risk 0.5 percent per trade, which is 25 USD. ATR(14) on the five-minute chart reads about 2.00, so a 1.5x ATR stop is 300 points. On one full lot, 300 points is roughly 300 USD of risk. Divide 25 by 300 and you get about 0.08 lots.

Now notice what happened. The stop got wider and the position got smaller, so the money at risk stayed exactly where you decided it would be. That is the whole trick. Confidence never enters the equation, which is precisely why it survives a bad mood.

| Input | Example value |
|---|---|
| Account equity | 5,000 USD |
| Risk per trade | 0.5% (25 USD) |
| ATR(14), M5 | ~2.00 |
| Stop distance | 1.5 x ATR = 300 points |
| Value per point, 1.00 lot | ~1 USD |
| Position size | ~0.08 lots |

Set this as a percentage in your EA's inputs rather than a fixed lot if you can. A fixed 0.10 lot is a different risk on a 1,000 USD account than on a 10,000 USD one, and it is a different risk in a quiet week than in an NFP week. Always round the result down, never up; rounding up is how a 0.5 percent risk quietly becomes 0.7 percent, and it is the most common way the maths gets undone in practice. The expert linked further down turns that calculation into an input — risk percentage in, lots out — so you are not doing the arithmetic by hand at 14:29.

One caution on the value-per-point figure. It depends on your contract size and account currency, and it can differ between brokers and between gold CFDs and spot gold. The example above assumes a standard 100-ounce lot quoted in US dollars. If your account is denominated in euros or pounds, or your broker uses a different contract size, confirm the figure before trusting the output. That is part of why a dedicated tool beats a mental estimate — the inputs are visible, so a wrong assumption can be seen and corrected.

## 5. Why does one position at a time beat three?

Because on gold, three "different" scalps in the same direction are usually one bet wearing three tickets. When XAUUSD drops 400 points, every one of those stops hits together, and the loss is three times what your risk plan expected.

Correlated exposure is the quiet account killer in scalping. You open a long, it goes against you, and instead of accepting the loss you add — and the second and third positions are not independent ideas, they are the same idea at worse prices. Margin usage climbs, the drawdown deepens, and the emotional pressure to "get it back" grows with it.

Discipline here is simple to state. One position per symbol. No averaging down into a loser. No stacking because the last signal "looked strong". Set it as a hard rule in the EA where possible, not as a preference you negotiate with yourself mid-session.

Margin tells the same story from the other side. Three gold positions at 0.1 lots each consume far more of your usable margin than one, and in a fast move the broker can close the weakest of them for you. That forced exit usually lands at the worst possible price, and it arrives exactly when you are least able to manage it. The cap protects you from that outcome as much as from your own decisions.

## 6. Why is a hard daily loss limit the rule you never negotiate?

A daily loss limit is the one rule that has to be decided in advance, because the day you need it is the day you will talk yourself out of it. Set it in money, wire it into the EA, and when the number is hit, trading stops — full stop.

The figure is personal, but 2–3 percent of equity per day is a common, defensible choice. On a 5,000 USD account that is 100 to 150 USD. The exact number matters less than three properties: it is written down, it is enforced automatically, and it is not editable at 16:00 when you are down and frustrated.

Why daily, and not just per trade? Because scalpers lose in clusters. One bad hour produces two losses, then a decision to size up, then two more. A per-trade limit cannot stop a spiral; a daily limit ends it by removing you from the screen. This is also where gold's volatility punishes the person who keeps going for "one more trade". Understanding that cluster behaviour is part of [risk and money management](/category/forex-trading-strategies/risk-money-management-forex-trading-strategies/), and it belongs in your settings, not in your willpower.

Implement the limit where you cannot override it easily. If your EA supports a daily loss input, use it. If it does not, set an account-level stop with your broker, or run the robot through a manager that enforces it. The point is to make the limit a property of the system rather than a decision you make while you are losing, because that is precisely when your judgement is worst.

Decide in advance what happens after the limit is hit. The useful answer is nothing — no more gold that day, and no "reviewing the charts" that quietly becomes a re-entry. Close the platform and come back tomorrow. Trading after a limit break is how a controlled loss turns into an uncontrolled one.

## 7. When should you re-test a gold scalping expert advisor?

Re-test on a written schedule — after any material change in volatility, after a broker or spread change, and at least once a quarter. Gold moves between calm regimes and violent ones, and settings tuned in a quiet month frequently fall apart in a CPI or NFP month.

The trap is treating a re-test as a one-off event you did when you installed the EA. Markets are not static inputs. A period of low volatility will make almost any scalper look smooth, and a single high-volatility week can erase a month of it if the stop and size assumptions no longer fit.

Put the dates in a calendar and keep a short log for each one:

- **Before the change:** note current ATR range, average spread, and drawdown.
- **During the review:** run the strategy tester on a recent sample, verifying rather than optimising.
- **After:** forward-test on a demo account for a set period before returning to live.
- **Record the outcome:** what changed, and whether the settings still match the current regime.

If you run several robots, do not re-test them all on the same day — stagger the dates so a single market event does not invalidate every conclusion at once.

Seasonality is part of this. Some months are simply quieter, and a scalper that looks steady in a low-volatility summer can struggle through an autumn of central-bank decisions. Your log should record the ATR range, not just the profit, so you can tell a real improvement from a lucky regime. A rising equity curve in a calm market is not evidence that the settings will survive a violent one.

Keep the schedule short and boring. A dated note once a quarter, plus a check after any change to your broker, spread or platform, is enough. What matters is that the re-test happens on a date, not on the day you finally feel worried — by which point the drawdown has usually already happened. ## What this tool does not do

Being honest about the limits is the difference between a tool and a promise, so here is the list.
