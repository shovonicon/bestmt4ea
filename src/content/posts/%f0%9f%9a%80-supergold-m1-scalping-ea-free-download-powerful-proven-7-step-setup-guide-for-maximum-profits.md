## The problem: on M1, the setup you can see is not the thing that decides your result

A one-minute gold chart looks like it should be easier to read. More candles, more signals, more opportunity. In practice, the M1 chart on XAUUSD is where costs become the dominant variable and where everything you cannot see on the chart — spread, slippage, latency, swap — does most of the work.

Start with spread, because it is charged on every trade regardless of direction. The London Bullion Market Association holds a twice-daily gold auction that gives the institutional market a reference price, but between auctions your broker streams its own blend of liquidity, and that blend has a spread that breathes. In a calm London hour on a raw account, XAUUSD might quote a round-trip spread around 15 to 30 points. Ten minutes into a US inflation release, the same quote can widen several-fold while price is still moving. A scalper taking a modest target pays that cost on every round trip, which means the trading day has a fixed tax that has to be cleared before any strategy edge is counted.

Now add stop distance. Gold moves further per unit time than any major currency pair. A stop sized for EURUSD — say 20 or 30 points — sits inside gold's normal noise and will be hit by moves that had nothing to do with your signal. The correct response is not a tighter stop. It is a wider stop, matched to how far gold actually travels, which in turn forces a smaller lot size to keep the same risk. Traders who cannot accept the smaller lot choose a tighter stop instead, and then wonder why the robot keeps getting "stopped out by noise", which is exactly what they built it to do.

Then multiply both problems by trade frequency. A one-minute scalper may fire dozens of times a day, and every one of those trades repeats the spread and the commission. A strategy with a thin per-trade edge can be entirely consumed by execution costs, and nothing in the EA's backtest numbers will show that clearly unless spread and commission were modelled honestly. The MQL5 tester's own documentation is careful about how it handles spread and tick generation precisely because those assumptions change results.

So the problem is not finding a SuperGold M1 scalping EA. There are endless files with names like that. The problem is that the variables which decide your outcome are set outside the EA: your broker, your risk input, your stop, and your discipline about hours. Get those right and almost any honest scalper has a chance. Get them wrong and no EA name will save you.

## Which three numbers should you set before an M1 gold scalper trades?

Three numbers do most of the work: stop distance in dollars of gold, risk per trade as a percentage of equity, and maximum spread in points. Set them in that order, because each one constrains the next.

| Input | M1 XAUUSD working range | Why that range | What goes wrong outside it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stop distance | 2.00 to 3.00 dollars (200 to 300 points on a two-decimal feed) | Wider than gold's ordinary M1 noise, still small enough to remain a scalp | Below 1.50 dollars the stop sits inside noise; above 5.00 dollars you are swing trading |
| Risk per trade | 0.25 to 0.5 percent of equity while learning | A five-loss run costs 1.25 to 2.5 percent, survivable | At 2 percent, five losses in a row is a 10 percent drawdown, and M1 produces runs like that |
| Maximum spread | 20 to 35 points, or the value you genuinely see most hours | Blocks the thin-book trades that erase the edge | Set to zero it disables the filter; set tighter than the market, the robot never trades |

The order matters. The stop is a property of the market — how far gold moves before a trade is decided — so you set it first, from measurement rather than hope. Risk is a property of your account, so you set it second, as a fixed fraction you are willing to lose on one idea. The lot size is not an input at all; it is the output of those two, calculated. Traders who pick a lot size first and then hunt for a stop that fits have chosen their risk by accident, and the market will eventually show them the number.

The spread cap deserves a special note because it is the most commonly sabotaged input. Setting it to zero feels like removing a restriction, but it does the opposite: it switches the filter off, so the robot trades through the exact thin-book windows — the minutes around data releases and the evening rollover — when the spread is widest. The honest setting is a number your broker quotes for the majority of your intended hours. That number will block trades. It should. A trade you never took costs nothing; a trade filled through a 90-point spread on a 200-point target is already behind when it opens.

There is one more variable that is not a number: the clock. Gold's spread is tightest and its ranges are widest in the London to New York overlap, roughly 09:00 to 16:30 London time on a normal day. The late evening and Asian hours combine a thinner book with a wider spread, which is a larger share of a scalping target. Restricting an M1 gold scalper to the liquid hours is one of the few settings that improves the odds without changing the strategy at all.

**Source code.** If the file is only `.ex4` or `.ex5`, you cannot see the strategy, the risk logic, or whether the stop is real. ## Why does the M1 timeframe punish weak risk management harder?

Because on M1 the number of decisions per day is high, and every decision carries a fixed cost and a chance to be wrong. Risk management is not a moral quality; it is the mechanism that keeps a normal run of losses from becoming a permanent one.

Consider the arithmetic of a losing streak. Any honest scalper loses several trades in a row regularly. At 0.5 percent risk per trade, a run of five losses is a 2.5 percent drawdown, which a normal month absorbs. At 2 percent risk, the same five-loss run is a 10 percent drawdown, and it will arrive in the first month, not the first year. The robot did not change. The size did. This is why experienced operators keep risk low and judge a scalper by whether its process is sound, not by whether a given week made money.

There is a second, subtler risk on M1: correlation of losses. A scalper that trades one symbol repeatedly can lose to the same condition multiple times in a row — a spread blowout, a single news spike, a burst of volatility that is not a trend. On gold, those cluster around the scheduled events the market watches: US CPI, Nonfarm Payrolls, and Federal Reserve decisions. A maximum daily loss limit and a rule to stand aside around those releases do more for survival than any improvement to the entry rule.

ESMA's product intervention measures cap leverage on gold at 20:1 for retail clients and require negative balance protection, which limits the worst case but does not remove it. No regulator sizes your positions. That part is arithmetic, and it is yours to get right before any expert advisor runs, free or otherwise.
