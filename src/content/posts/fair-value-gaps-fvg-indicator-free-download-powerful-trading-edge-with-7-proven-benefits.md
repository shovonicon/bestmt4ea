## **What Are Fair Value Gaps (FVG)?**

Fair Value Gaps, often called FVGs, represent price imbalances in the market. These gaps occur when price moves aggressively in one direction, leaving an area where little to no trading activity happened.

In simple terms? The market moved too fast, and price left “unfinished business” behind.

This concept became popular through Smart Money Concepts (SMC), largely taught by institutional-style educators like Michael Huddleston. The idea is that institutions create these imbalances when executing large orders.

### **The Origin of FVG in Smart Money Concepts**

Big financial institutions don’t enter trades like retail traders. They place large orders that push price strongly, creating inefficiencies. These inefficiencies are what we call Fair Value Gaps.

Later, price often returns to these gaps to rebalance before continuing its move.

### **Why Fair Value Gaps Matter in Trading**

Markets seek efficiency. When price leaves an imbalance, it tends to revisit that area. Traders use FVG zones as:

-   Entry points
-   Take-profit targets
-   Stop-loss placement guides
-   Confluence zones

This gives structure to what might otherwise feel like random price movement.
