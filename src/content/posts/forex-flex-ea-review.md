## What is Forex Flex EA?

Forex Flex EA is a commercial expert advisor for MetaTrader 4 (MT4) and MetaTrader 5 (MT5), sold by its developer through the website forexflexea.com. The vendor sells MT4 and MT5 versions separately or as a bundle, and says it works with "any MT4/MT5 forex broker".

The vendor's page presents it as a multi-strategy system. It lists dozens of selectable strategies, hundreds of unique settings and hundreds of set files. A set file is a saved list of input values that you load into the EA so you do not enter every setting by hand. The vendor's headline also says "Best MT4 EA since 2012", and the page refers to results from 2012 to 2022. An independent review by NYCServers repeats that the EA has been in development since 2012, but it takes that from the developer. We could not find an independent source that confirms the start year, so treat it as the vendor's claim.

Independent coverage is thin. The reviews we found fall into three groups: the vendor's own pages, affiliate-style review sites, and a review from a company that sells VPS hosting to Forex Flex EA users. One search summary we ran noted that Forextester and Forex Peace Army state outright that they do not endorse products like this. In short, nobody outside the vendor has published a controlled forward test that we could find.

That does not make the EA bad. It means the weight of proof sits with the buyer, and the rest of this review is about how to carry it.

## How does Forex Flex EA trade?

The vendor's page describes four working parts. We list them as the vendor words them, because the exact wording matters when you ask questions later.

**Virtual trades.** The EA runs simulated trades in the background, six per pair by default, and uses them to find entries. The idea is that it watches how simulated positions behave before it opens a real one. The NYCServers review describes this as assessing momentum and volatility. Both descriptions come from the developer. We cannot say whether this improves entries, because we have no controlled comparison against the same strategy without the virtual stage.

**Equity trailing take-profit.** Trades on a pair are grouped into a basket. Once the basket reaches a preset percentage of profit, the vendor says half of that profit is locked in and the stop then trails upward. This means the EA judges a group of positions together instead of giving each one its own fixed take-profit.

**Drawdown stop loss.** When drawdown reaches a set percentage of the balance, the EA closes all trades. Drawdown is the drop from a peak in your account equity to a later low. A drawdown stop loss is an emergency exit for the whole basket. Where you set it is the most important setting you will choose, and we come back to it below.

**Hedging.** The vendor says hedging is off by default. Hedging means holding opposite positions at the same time.

The NYCServers review adds claims that come from the developer: more than 15 built-in indicators (it names EMA, TDI, RSI and Bollinger Bands), news filters that pause trading around high-impact events such as NFP and FOMC, session filters for London, New York and Asia, dynamic lot sizing from balance or equity, and automatic updates with optimised settings.

### Is it a grid or a martingale?

This is the question we would ask first, and the vendor's page does not answer it. The page does not describe Forex Flex EA as a grid or martingale system. The only mention of grids that we found is the name of one showcased account, "HalfGrid". A grid system opens extra positions at set price steps. A martingale raises position size after a loss. Both can produce smooth equity curves for a long time and then a very large loss.

Because the EA offers dozens of strategies, the honest answer is probably "it depends on which strategy and settings you load". That is your cue to ask the vendor, in writing, how each strategy you plan to use adds positions and what the maximum number of open trades and the largest lot size reached in the tracked accounts were. Then check the Myfxbook trade history yourself. We show how below.

### What does the vendor recommend for pairs and timeframes?

The recommended pairs on the vendor's page include GBPUSD, USDJPY, EURUSD and AUDUSD, and the page says some pairs are best avoided. It also says the EA can run on "virtually any charts". The showcased accounts are all on the H1 chart. Pair choice changes spread, volatility and how often the EA trades, so follow the vendor's recommendation for your first test instead of trying everything at once.

## What does the track record actually show?

The vendor's page shows about ten Myfxbook accounts. Myfxbook is a third-party service that connects to a trading account and publishes its history, so the vendor cannot edit the numbers. That is better evidence than a screenshot. It is still the vendor's choice of which accounts to show.

On the page, as we could read it, the vendor makes two specific claims. One account, "x3Retrace", is described as having reached 300% before the owner withdrew all the money. Another, "Default 6 Month 3x", is described as having tripled in six months. The gain and drawdown numbers themselves sit inside embedded widgets that our text reading could not capture, so we are not quoting any percentages from them. Open the accounts yourself and read the figures at the source.

The NYCServers reviewer notes that results can differ depending on when the EA was started, because it uses CSV files for its analysis. The reviewer also says some verified accounts have shown significant drawdowns, that exact Myfxbook performance should not be expected to repeat, and that the EA carries real risk. We would add: every EA carries risk, and a review that says so is more useful than one that does not.

### How do you read a Myfxbook page for a system like this?

Do not scan for the biggest green number. Open each account and write down these items.

| Check | What to look for | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Account type | Demo or live | A demo fill does not carry the slippage and requotes a live account has. |
| Verified status | Trading privileges verified and a connected broker | An unverified account can be edited or reset. |
| Age | Months and years of history | Three months of gains is a streak. Three years is a record. |
| Absolute gain vs gain | Both figures, and the deposits and withdrawals | A withdrawal can make an account look like it lost; a large deposit can make it look like it gained. |
| Maximum drawdown | Percentage and date | This is the number your nerves and your account size must survive. |
| Trade count | Hundreds or thousands | Ten trades prove nothing. |
| Largest lot and open trades | Peak position size and the most trades open at once | These expose grid or martingale behaviour. |
| Settings used | A named set file and a risk level | A result is only repeatable with the same settings. |
| Start date | When the EA began on that account | A curve that starts right after a bad period flatters the result. |

If the page does not tell you the settings behind a tracked account, ask. A track record you cannot reproduce is a story, not a test.

### What about the claim that an account "reached 300% then withdrew all the money"?

Read it carefully. It says an account reached a large gain and that the owner then took the money out. It does not tell you the starting balance, the drawdown on the way, the time it took, the lot size, or whether the account was live. A withdrawal is also the end of that record, which means the account does not show how the EA behaved afterwards. These are the questions to put to the vendor, not reasons to dismiss the claim.

### What is the bias to watch for in any vendor track record?

The vendor decides which accounts to display. Accounts that did badly are not on the page. One source reviewing the prop firm setups noted that the setups shown are the only passing accounts, so no pass rate can be calculated. The same logic applies to the main page. Ten displayed accounts are not ten tests; they are the ones that made the page.

The fix is to ask for the list of all accounts run with the same strategy, including the failed ones. If the vendor refuses, that is an answer.

## Does Forex Flex EA pass prop firm challenges?

The vendor sells a "Prop Firm EA Version" and includes it in the $499.95 package. Its FAQ says many users have reported passing challenges and points to a separate FTMO-focused product. It gives no pass counts, no failure counts and no dates.

A prop firm challenge is a paid evaluation. You trade a simulated account and must reach a profit target without breaking a daily loss or total loss limit. Those limits are strict and they change. A basket EA with a drawdown stop loss can fit that design, but "can fit" is not "does pass".

If you want to use it for a challenge, do this.

1. **Read the prop firm's current rules** for daily loss, maximum loss, news trading, weekend holding and EA use. Rules change, so do not rely on a review for them.
2. **Match the EA's drawdown stop loss to the prop firm's limit.** The stop must trigger well inside the firm's daily and total loss limit, with room for slippage.
3. **Demo test the exact set file** under the challenge's rules before paying the challenge fee.
4. **Ask for pass and fail numbers** from the vendor for that exact version and firm, and treat anything vague as unknown.

The challenge fee is a real cost on top of the licence. Do not buy the licence and the challenge on the same day.

## What account size and broker do you need?

The vendor's stated minimum deposits are $10 on a nano account, $100 on a micro or cent account, and $1,000 on a standard account. The NYCServers review does not state a minimum balance, and says traders with very small accounts may struggle to absorb drawdowns.

We read those numbers as the lowest balance the software will accept, not the balance at which a basket strategy is comfortable. A basket EA can hold several positions at once, and each position has a minimum lot size at your broker. On a small balance, the smallest lot can already be a large share of your equity when several trades are open.

A simple way to check this is to set the drawdown stop loss at the level you could actually accept, then ask what lot size keeps the combined loss of a full basket under that figure. If the answer is below your broker's minimum lot, the account is too small for that strategy.

For the broker, the NYCServers reviewer recommends low-spread ECN accounts with fast execution. A scalping or short-term basket system is sensitive to spread and slippage, so run your demo test on the same broker and account type you would use live. The NYCServers reviewer recommends a VPS for steady uptime and lower latency, and argues the virtual-trade engine needs continuous data. That reviewer works for a VPS provider, so factor in the interest. Still, the logic holds for most EAs: if your computer sleeps, your EA stops. ## How do you test Forex Flex EA before spending real money?

This is the section that protects your money. The vendor and the NYCServers review both say unlimited demo use is included. Use it.

Open a demo account at the broker and account type you would use live. Set the balance to the amount you plan to trade. Do not test with a $100,000 demo balance and then trade $500.

Follow this plan.

| Week | What to do | What to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Install the EA, load one vendor-recommended set file, and write down every input value. | Settings, balance, broker, account type, date. |
| 1 | Run on one recommended pair and the H1 chart. Change nothing. | Daily balance, equity, open trades, largest lot. |
| 2 | Keep running. Note the first drawdown and how the basket behaved. | Maximum drawdown so far, number of trades. |
| 3 to 4 | Keep settings fixed. Compare with the tracked account's equivalent weeks. | Gap between your results and the tracked account. |
| 5 to 6 | Run through a high-impact news event. Check the news filter behaved as described. | Spread at the news, trades opened or paused. |
| 7 to 8 | Decide using your numbers: drawdown against your limit, the gap against the tracked account, and your own comfort. | One-page summary. |

Rules for the test.

- **Change nothing mid-test.** If you change a setting, you have started a new test.
- **Write down your stop condition before you begin.** For example: "If demo drawdown passes X%, I do not buy."
- **Compare with the tracked account honestly.** A gap is information. Large gaps between your demo and the vendor's account usually come from different brokers, spreads, start dates or settings.
- **Treat a good demo as a reason to continue, not proof.** Demo fills do not carry the slippage and requotes of a live account.
- **Start live small, if you start.** Use a balance you could lose, and keep the lot size low.

For more on testing methods, read our guide on [backtesting versus forward testing an EA](/backtesting-vs-forward-testing-verify-ea/). The MetaTrader documentation on [testing trading strategies](https://www.metatrader5.com/en/terminal/help/algotrading/testing) explains the tester's limits.
