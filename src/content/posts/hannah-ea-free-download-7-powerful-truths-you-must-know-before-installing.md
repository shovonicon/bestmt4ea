## Truth one: "free" commercial software is a transaction, and you are the payment

A cracked EA is not a gift economy artefact. The sites distributing commercial robots without charge monetise somehow, and the menu of methods is short: advertising at best, bundled installers and activators in the middle, and at the ugly end, loaders whose payload is aimed at exactly the machine a trader uses — the one with broker credentials saved, payment sessions logged in, and a user trained to click through warnings because the prize seems worth it. Security researchers find this pattern wherever valuable software is pirated, and trading software is a connoisseur's target because a compromised trading machine can be monetised in several currencies at once. Even where no malware is present, the file's integrity is unknowable: risk parameters can be altered, account numbers can be harvested by logic you cannot read in a compiled .ex4, and "the EA went crazy and emptied the account" is a story with a cracked build somewhere in its ancestry more often than its tellers know.

## Truth two: the claims that make it desirable are the claims that demand proof

Hannah EA's publicity belongs to a recognisable genre: very high win rates, rapid account growth, set-and-forget language. Read those claims the way a reviewer must. A high win rate is compatible with losing money whenever average losses exceed average wins, which is the normal shape of the systems that advertise win rate loudest. Rapid growth claims are compatible with enormous risk quietly compounding until it does not. And "fully automatic" is compatible with any behaviour at all, including behaviour you would never have approved in writing. None of this proves the product is bad. It proves the claims are unpriced: without an independently verified track record — real account, open history, drawdown visible — they are marketing sentences, and marketing sentences are not a basis for installing anything on a machine that holds your money.

## Truth three: a cracked build freezes you in the product's worst week

Commercial EAs are moving targets. Vendors patch brokers' changes, fix bugs, adjust to platform updates. A pirated copy is a photograph of one build, often chosen by the cracker for ease rather than quality, and it ages from the day you install it. The original owners who hit a problem get an update; you get a forum thread. Worse, you cannot distinguish product faults from crack faults, so every malfunction is undiagnosable — and as our troubleshooting guide describes, an undiagnosable EA problem tends to be solved by users changing risk settings, which is how a free download becomes an expensive one in a second, slower way.

## Truth four: the broker is part of the software, whether the licence likes it or not

Unauthorised EAs can breach more than the vendor's terms. Brokers prohibit certain practices — some restrict particular high-frequency or latency strategies, many treat account access by unknown third-party code as the account holder's responsibility in full — and "the file did it" is not a defence that recovers a balance. Using software whose provenance you cannot state, on an account whose terms you accepted, concentrates every liability on the one party with no support contract: you. ## Inside the tampering: how a cracked EA is actually altered

It is worth understanding mechanically what a "cracked" build is, because the risks stop being abstract once the plumbing is visible. A commercial EA is protected in two places: in its code, where licence checks ask a server or a stored key whether this account may run it, and in its distribution, where the file is supposed to travel only from vendor to customer. Cracking attacks the first of those. Sometimes the licence check inside the compiled file is patched — instructions altered so the check always answers yes. Sometimes the EA is shipped with a loader or "activator", a separate program that intercepts the check from outside, which is why activators so often demand administrator rights and ask you to disable your antivirus before running: the honest read of that request is that the software intends to do things your machine's defences are designed to prevent, and it needs you to stand the guards down first.

Each method hands the person doing the tampering complete opportunity to add cargo of their own. A patched file can carry extra logic that is invisible in the trading behaviour for months: a small percentage of trades mirrored to another account, risk defaults quietly raised so the account trades larger than the user set, an instruction that activates only on a particular date or balance, or data sent out through the terminal's permitted channels to an address the user never sees named anywhere. A bundled installer has even freer rein, because it runs with the user's full cooperation during setup: password stealers aimed at browsers where broker logins are saved, clipboard watchers that wait for a wallet address or account number to be copied, remote-access tools installed "for support". The trading machine is the worst machine in the house to compromise, since it unites saved financial credentials, logged-in payment sessions and a user accustomed to executing files from strangers. None of this requires the original EA to be malicious; the vendor's code may be entirely honest. The poison, when there is poison, is added in the one step the downloader asked for — the step that broke the protection. Two features of this trade make detection unlikely. First, a compiled expert advisor is opaque by design: you cannot open an .ex4 in a text editor and audit it, so the downloader has no practical means of verifying that the file does what the original did, and must extend total trust to the least trustworthy link in the chain. Second, the damage is usually deniable: an account drained by altered logic looks like an account lost to bad trading, and a credential stolen in March may be used in October, by which time the download is forgotten. The downloader who insists nothing bad happened after their last crack is making a claim about a system designed so that they would be the last to know.

The tampering also compounds over time, because a cracked user still wants what ownership provides: updates. The distribution sites know this, and "new version available" is a renewable excuse to deliver a fresh installer to an audience that has already demonstrated it will run one. Each update cycle re-runs the original gamble with a new unknown file, on a machine the user has progressively trained — exclusions added, warnings waved through — to accept it. Genuine ownership concentrates its trust once, in a vendor whose business depends on not abusing it. The cracked arrangement re-spends that trust indefinitely, on anonymous uploaders whose business depends on nothing of the sort.

## The legal position, stated plainly

This section is general information about how the rules typically work, not legal advice about any individual's circumstances, but the broad shape is consistent enough across jurisdictions to be worth stating. Software is generally licensed, not sold: buying an EA grants you a right to use it under the vendor's terms, and those terms almost invariably prohibit copying, redistribution and circumventing the licence mechanism. Downloading a cracked copy therefore typically puts the user in breach of the licence and, in many places, on the wrong side of copyright law as well, quite apart from the separate offences involved in distributing it. Anti-circumvention rules — the provisions that make it unlawful to bypass technical protection measures, found in laws such as the United States' DMCA and their equivalents elsewhere — mean that the activator itself can be an infringing tool even before the EA is run once. Enforcement against individual downloaders of trading software is uncommon, and honesty requires saying so; but uncommon is a description of the vendor's enforcement budget, not of the downloader's legal position, and traders with regulated employers, professional licences or prop-firm contracts can find that a casual infringement has professional consequences out of all proportion to the saving.

The less obvious exposure runs through contracts rather than courts. Broker terms make the account holder responsible for activity on the account, including activity by third-party software the holder installed; a broker that identifies unauthorised or tampered code behind abusive trading patterns is entitled under most agreements to close positions and the account. Prop firms are stricter still, since their rules typically require that trading be the customer's own and conducted with permitted tools. Payment adds a final layer of grubbiness: the grey-market resellers who sell "cheap licences" of cracked products operate outside every refund mechanism their victims might appeal to, accepting payment methods chosen precisely because the charge cannot be reversed. The through-line of all of it is asymmetry. The saving from a pirated EA is bounded and known — the product's price. The liabilities it creates are unbounded and unknowable in advance. Adults are free to accept bad bargains, but they should at least see both sides of this one written down before they accept it.

## Marketing-claims literacy: reading a sales page like an auditor

Since the desire for this product begins with its marketing, the durable skill is learning to audit the marketing itself — a skill that transfers to every EA you will ever consider, including the expensive ones sold entirely legitimately. Start with the equity curve, the genre's centrepiece. A smooth curve rising at a steady angle with shallow, brief dips is the signature not of a good system but of a particular risk shape: one that wins small amounts very often and defers its losses, by holding losing positions, widening their room, or adding to them, until some are rescued by the market's ordinary wandering. Such curves are real right up to the day they are not, and the information that would price the risk — the deepest floating drawdown the account carried, the largest the basket of open losers ever grew, the margin peak — is exactly what a cropped statement screenshot omits. Train yourself to ask for the valleys between the statement's dates, not just the statement. Track-record language deserves the same parsing. On the major tracking platforms, there is a material difference between a record whose trading privileges have been verified — evidence the uploader actually controls the account — and one merely uploaded; between a record with full history open and one with open trades, lot sizes or history hidden; and between a live account and a demo wearing live clothing. Each concealment option exists for a reason, and a vendor who uses several of them at once while quoting the resulting numbers to three significant figures is telling you how the numbers were made. Period selection is the quieter art: systems have seasons, and a curve that begins the month after a strategy's worst drawdown and ends the month before its second-worst is a true statement arranged to imply a false one. The auditor's counter-question is always the same: the whole history, please, including the beginning, when the account was small and the vendor's incentives were the same as yours.

Finally, read the commercial terms as part of the evidence, because they are the one part of the page the vendor had to write carefully. A refund policy with conditions that make refunds practically unattainable, a licence tied to a single account number with paid changes, support that exists only as a sales chat — these are data about how the vendor expects the ownership experience to go. Compare like with like: serious vendors in this market state their strategy in words a competent trader can repeat back, name their drawdowns without being cornered, and sell at prices that assume the customer will test before judging. Hannah EA's publicity, measured by these standards, leans on the dramatic end of every scale — growth, win rate, effortlessness — while the verifiable record behind it remains, at the time of writing, unavailable to us. That combination is not proof of a bad product. It is proof that the burden of evidence has not been met, and the correct response to an unmet burden is a demo test or a closed wallet, in that order.

## If you already downloaded one: a damage-limitation sequence

Some readers arrive here with the file already on their machine, occasionally already run. This section is for them, and it is written without scolding, because the useful thing now is sequence rather than regret. The first step is simply to stop: do not attach the file to a live account "just to see", and do not run the activator again to fix an error it reported — error messages are the standard pretext for getting a victim to disable one more protection and run one more payload. If the EA is on a chart, remove it; if a position it opened is live, close or manage that position yourself in the ordinary way, because account hygiene starts with knowing which open risk is yours.

Next, treat the machine as untrusted until it has earned back your confidence. Run a full scan with your security software restored to its proper settings — if you were persuaded to add exclusions or switch protection off, undo those changes first, since they are the holes anything else would use. Then, from a different, clean device, change the passwords that matter: your broker account password and investor communications, the email account those resets travel through, and any payment service used on the trading machine. Changing passwords from the possibly compromised machine itself is locking a door while standing in the room with the intruder; use the other device. Enable whatever additional sign-in protection your broker and email provider offer, and check the account's recent access and withdrawal destinations for anything you do not recognise. In the terminal itself, review the settings that govern what code may do: which addresses automated web requests may contact, whether external libraries are permitted, and which accounts the terminal remembers. A tampered setup often leaves one of these doors propped open.

Finally, remove the whole installation rather than attempting to disinfect the EA file alone: uninstall, delete the downloaded archive and its installer from wherever they landed, including the browser's download folder and any "portable" terminal copy the bundle created, and reinstall MetaTrader from your broker's own link. Keep notes of what you did and when. If nothing was wrong, you have spent an hour on hygiene that does no harm. If something was wrong, the notes are what a broker's support team or a fraud department will ask for first, and the hour will look very well spent. Then return to the legitimate routes this article describes: the free shelf for learning, the vendor for the genuine product after a workbook test, and a machine whose only surprises come from the market, which provides quite enough of those unaided.

One habit prevents the sequel: separate the machine's roles where you can. Trading credentials, payment sessions and experimental downloads do not belong in the same environment, and a dedicated browser profile for broker and banking access — never used for forums, file sites or installers — removes most of the prize that makes a trading computer worth attacking in the first place. Security advice is unfashionable right up until the afternoon it becomes biography; this is the cheap, boring kind that tends to work. Treat any future urge to test an unknown file as a signal to use a demo account on a clean install instead: curiosity is a legitimate research tool, but it should be exercised where the worst outcome is a pretend loss and a useful note in a testing log, not a real balance and a difficult telephone call.
