# The Concept of Fair Value Gap (FVG) Explained

## Understanding Fair Value Gaps (FVG) in Trading

A Fair Value Gap occurs when there is a strong move in price that creates a gap between candles. This typically happens during high volatility events like:

-   Major economic news
-   Institutional order blocks
-   Liquidity sweeps

In simple terms, an FVG is a price imbalance. Markets tend to revisit these zones before continuing in the original direction.

## Market Imbalance and Institutional Trading

Large institutions don’t enter trades like retail traders. They execute massive orders, creating rapid price movements. These moves leave inefficiencies — and that’s what FVG traders target.

Smart money strategies often combine FVG with:

-   Market structure shifts
-   Break of structure (BOS)
-   Liquidity grabs

## Three-Candle Pattern Structure

The classic FVG pattern uses three candles:

-   Candle 1: Strong impulse
-   Candle 2: Large expansion candle
-   Candle 3: Leaves a gap between candle 1 and candle 3

### Bullish FVG Example

4

In a bullish FVG:

-   Price rallies strongly.
-   A gap forms between the first candle’s high and third candle’s low.
-   Price later retraces into the gap before moving upward again.

### Bearish FVG Example

4

In a bearish FVG:

-   Price drops aggressively.
-   A gap forms between the first candle’s low and third candle’s high.
-   Price retraces into the imbalance and continues downward.
