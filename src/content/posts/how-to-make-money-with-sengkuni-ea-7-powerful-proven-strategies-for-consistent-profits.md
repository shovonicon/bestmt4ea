## What Does an Expert Advisor Automate, and What Does It Leave to You?

An expert advisor (EA) is a program that runs inside MetaTrader. It watches prices, checks conditions against rules written by its developer, and sends orders to your broker when those conditions are met. It can also modify stop losses, close positions and track its own results. Think of it as a checklist that never gets tired, applied to every tick the terminal receives.

What it does not do is equally important. It does not decide how much of your account to risk unless its developer built that logic in and you accept its defaults. It does not know whether a news release is about to happen. It does not notice that your broker has changed its minimum stop distance last week. It does not stop itself after a run of losses unless it was programmed to. It does not know your personal limits. Those jobs stay with you, and many of them are the difference between a controlled test and a runaway account.

Sengkuni EA is described in its listing as an automated system that uses pre-programmed logic to enter and exit trades on forex pairs. Typical descriptions of this type of tool mention price action, technical indicators, volatility and trend analysis. Those are categories, not verified specifications. You need to read the program's own documentation, look at its input parameters inside the platform and check the trade log after a demo run to understand what it actually does.

### Why the Developer's Description Is Only a Starting Point

Sales pages describe what a program is meant to do. They rarely explain how it behaves when the spread doubles, the broker rejects an order, or the price gaps through a stop. A better question than "what does it do?" is "what happens when it cannot do what it is designed for?" Ask that question of any expert advisor, and write the answer down before you test it.

### How Do Settings Change the Behaviour?

Most expert advisors expose inputs. These are the values you can change before the program starts, such as lot size, maximum open trades, stop distance, target distance, session filters and risk percentage. Small changes can alter the behaviour dramatically. A larger lot size increases every outcome. A tighter stop may cut losses early but get hit by normal noise. A session filter can keep the program out of quiet hours, or it can leave it trading when spreads are poor. Treat every input as a decision you can explain, not a number you copied from a forum.

## Why Does the Platform Setup Matter More Than the Name?

The platform setup decides whether the expert advisor runs at all. In MetaTrader, automated trading must be allowed in the terminal and on the individual program. If auto-trading is switched off, the program will appear on the chart but will not place orders. A common mistake is to attach the program, see a smiley face on the chart, and assume it is working. The best way to confirm it is to check the Experts tab and the Journal tab for messages after each session.

The second requirement is continuity. A terminal that is asleep, disconnected or restarted will stop managing open trades. Rina's story above shows what that looks like in practice. If you run the program on a home computer, check the power settings, the operating system updates and the internet connection before you rely on it. If you cannot keep the computer online for long periods, consider a virtual private server. A [VPS](/product/vps/) can keep the terminal running around the clock, but it is only a hosting solution. It does not improve the strategy and it does not remove the need to monitor logs.

Make sure the EA version matches your platform. MetaTrader 4 and MetaTrader 5 use different languages and different file formats, so a program built for one will not run on the other. Confirm the platform before you download. If the listing is vague about which version it supports, treat that as a reason to look elsewhere, or to ask the publisher directly.

### Verify Auto-Trading Before Every Session

Build a short routine. Before a session starts, confirm that the terminal is connected, that the auto-trading button is enabled, that the program is attached to the correct chart and that the input settings match your test plan. After the session, read the Journal and Experts logs for errors, rejected orders, reconnect messages and any warnings about trade permissions. Logs are dull, which is why most traders skip them. They are also the fastest way to discover that something is wrong before it costs money.

## How Do You Size Positions So One Bad Week Stays Survivable?

Position sizing is the setting most likely to decide whether an automated account survives. Many traders assume the strategy is the important part and the lot size is a detail. In practice, the lot size decides how big a normal losing streak feels, and whether you can stay disciplined through it.

Use a simple rule. Calculate the amount you are willing to lose on a single trade as a percentage of your account, often one to two percent, and work backwards from your stop distance to find the lot size that keeps the loss within that limit. Do this before you let the program trade live. Many expert advisors have a fixed lot setting. That is convenient, but it means the risk per trade changes whenever your balance changes, and it does not adjust to the distance of the stop. If the program uses a fixed lot size, recalculate the risk after every significant change in balance.

Set a daily loss limit as well. A daily limit stops the program, or you, from continuing after a bad session. Some expert advisors have a built-in drawdown setting. Use it if it exists, and verify that it works by testing it on demo. A safety feature that has never been triggered in testing is a feature you do not understand yet.

### Leverage and Margin

Leverage lets a small deposit control a large position. It also makes every price move feel larger in your account balance. A moderate leverage setting gives the program room to breathe and gives you room to react. Very high leverage makes a normal fluctuation dangerous. Check your broker's margin rules, and remember that a margin call can close positions regardless of what the program intended. Keep enough free margin to survive a normal adverse move without the broker stepping in.

## How Do You Backtest Without Fooling Yourself?

Backtesting runs the expert advisor against historical price data. MetaTrader includes a Strategy Tester that can do this. A good backtest shows you how the program would have behaved in the past under the conditions you set. It is useful for finding obvious faults, such as trades opened at the wrong time or stops placed in impossible positions. It is not a forecast, and it can be dangerously misleading.

The most common error is curve fitting. This happens when settings are adjusted again and again until the historical results look excellent, and the adjustments only work on the data used to choose them. A strategy that has been tuned to one year of data may fail in a different year. To reduce this risk, split your history into two parts. Optimise on the first part, then test the chosen settings on the second part without changing anything. If performance falls apart on the untouched period, the settings were fitted to noise.

Model quality matters too. The tester can simulate ticks in different ways, and the model you choose changes the result. Use the highest tick quality your data supports, include realistic spreads and note any commission assumptions. Otherwise you may be testing a version of the market that does not exist.

Finally, look beyond the net profit line. Examine the maximum drawdown, the longest losing streak, the average trade, the number of trades, the profit factor and how the equity curve moves over time. A flat-looking profit with deep, long drawdowns can be harder to live with than a steady small loss. Ask whether you would have kept running the program through the worst stretch of the test. If the honest answer is no, the risk is too high for you, whatever the total profit says.

## How Should You Run a Forward Demo Test?

Forward testing means running the expert advisor on live market data in real time, on a demo account, without changing its settings. It is the step that shows whether the backtest was a fair picture. Markets change, brokers change and spreads move, so the forward results often differ from the history. That difference is information you need.

Set the demo test up like a real trade. Use the same platform, the same VPS or always-on computer, the same broker you plan to use and the same lot sizing. Run the test for several weeks, ideally across different sessions and at least one major news event. Keep a log of every trade with the entry time, spread at entry, stop, target, slippage and result. Compare the demo results with your backtest expectations at the end of each week, not just at the end of the test.

Watch for the signs of a fragile system. Trades that open at prices far from the intended level suggest slippage problems. Frequent rejected orders suggest broker or execution issues. Long periods with no trades may mean the filters are too strict, or that the platform is not connected. Clusters of trades in a short window may point to an entry rule that fires repeatedly on the same move. Each of these needs an explanation before you go live. A weekly review helps more than a daily glance. Once a week, export the trade history, compare the average spread against your backtest assumption, count the rejected or modified orders, and note any trade that closed for a reason you did not expect. Put the findings in a short document with the date and the settings used. If the settings change during the test, start a new entry rather than editing the old one, so you can tell which version produced which result. This habit also protects you from a common trap, where a good demo week leads you to raise the lot size or relax a filter before the evidence supports it. Judge each change by the next full week of data, not by the mood of the last few trades.

### How Long Should a Demo Test Run?

Several weeks is a practical minimum, but the right length depends on how many trades the program takes. A program that trades a few times per month needs a longer test to produce any useful sample. Keep testing until you have seen several market moods, including a quiet week, a volatile week and at least one news-heavy period. A demo run that only covers the good days proves very little.

## How Do Broker Conditions Change the Result?

Broker conditions are one of the least discussed causes of poor automated results. The spread is the difference between the buy and sell prices, and it is a cost on every trade. Commission may apply in addition. Execution speed determines how close your fill is to the price the program requested. Slippage occurs when the fill differs from the requested price, often during fast markets. Some brokers also restrict hedging, scalping or certain order types, which can break a strategy outright.

Before you go live, read your broker's terms and write down the spread, commission, minimum stop distance, typical execution behaviour and any restrictions on automated trading. Compare the broker's conditions with the assumptions in your backtest. If the backtest used a fixed spread of one pip and your broker averages two, the strategy you tested is not the strategy you will trade.

| Broker factor | Why it matters for an EA | What to check before going live |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spread | A direct cost on every entry and exit | Average spread at your session times |
| Commission | Adds to the cost of each trade | Whether it applies to your account type |
| Execution speed | Affects fill price and slippage | Slippage in your demo logs |
| Minimum stop distance | Can reject stops the program sets | Broker specification for your pair |
| Hedging rules | Can block some multi-position logic | Written terms from the broker |
| Leverage and margin | Sets how quickly a drawdown becomes a margin problem | Margin level rules and stop-out level |

The table is a checklist, not a verdict. Your broker may be excellent for one kind of program and poor for another. The point is to make the conditions visible before they affect your money.

## What Should You Do When High-Impact News Arrives?

News is one of the most reliable ways to break an automated strategy. Economic releases can move prices sharply within seconds. Spreads widen before and after the release, and orders can fill far from the requested price. A program that works well in calm conditions may open a position just before a release and then take a large loss when volatility spikes.

Use an economic calendar. Before each trading session, check which high-impact events are scheduled, and decide in advance whether the expert advisor will trade around them. You can pause the program manually, disable it during set windows, or use a news filter if the program includes one. Test the news behaviour on demo. A news filter that has never been exercised in testing is a feature you cannot trust.

Keep in mind that a news pause does not remove all risk. Positions already open when a release arrives can still move against you. Decide ahead of time how you will handle open trades during news, and whether the program should close them, leave them or reduce exposure.

## What Are Common Mistakes With Automated Trading?

Several mistakes appear repeatedly across accounts. Traders run the program on a live account before finishing a demo test. They change settings after every losing trade, which means they never test any one version properly. They ignore the logs, and then wonder why orders were rejected. They raise the lot size after a good week, so the next bad week hurts more. They assume that because the program is automated, they no longer need a plan for drawdown.

The most damaging mistake is treating automation as permission to stop paying attention. A program is a tool that repeats your rules. If those rules are untested, mis-sized or poorly matched to the broker, the repetition makes the damage faster. Keep reviewing the results, keep the risk limits in place and be willing to switch the program off.
