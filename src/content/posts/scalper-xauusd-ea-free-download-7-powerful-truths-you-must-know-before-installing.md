## Truth two: the stop logic is the strategy

On a gold scalper, how it loses is more important than how it wins, because losing is what ends accounts. Open the source or the inputs and confirm two things: that a stop-loss is set on every position, and that there is no recovery logic of any kind.

You do not need to read MQL to screen for this. Search the source for the order function that opens positions, and for words like `LotMultiplier`, `GridStep`, `Martingale` or `Averaging`. A stop that is genuinely set will appear as a stop-loss level applied after the order opens. Recovery logic appears as a growing position count or a multiplying lot size, and both mean the EA is hiding losses in floating exposure rather than accepting them.

Gold is the worst possible instrument for recovery logic, because it trends harder and further than a currency pair. The LBMA and CME price drivers — inflation data, employment data, rate decisions, the dollar index — frequently push gold several percent in a session. A grid built for small steps meets a large directional day, and the arithmetic that looked fine in a range becomes the mechanism of the loss. ## Truth three: the spread it needs decides whether it works

A scalper's edge is not the signal; it is the signal minus the cost of taking it. On gold, that cost is the round-trip spread plus commission, charged on every trade, and it is the reason the same robot can be positive on one broker and negative on another.

Here is the arithmetic as it actually plays out. On a raw account in a calm London hour, XAUUSD might quote a round-trip spread around 15 to 30 points. Around a US inflation release, the same symbol can quote several times that while price is still moving, because liquidity thins when everyone wants the same side. A scalper targeting a modest range pays that cost on every trade, so the number of trades multiplies the tax. A robot that fires fifty times a month pays it fifty times.

That is why the maximum spread input is a risk feature, not a nicety. Set it to a value your broker genuinely quotes for most of your intended hours, and accept that it blocks trades. A blocked trade you had no edge on costs nothing. A trade filled through a 100-point spread on a 200-point target is already behind when it opens. And setting the cap to zero does not widen your tolerance; it switches the filter off, which is the opposite of what the input is for. ## Truth four: the drawdown figure is the one that decides survival

A win rate tells you how often trades worked; a drawdown tells you how bad the bad weeks get. On gold, the second number is the one that determines whether you are still trading next year.

When you look at any drawdown claim, ask three questions: is it measured on equity or balance, over how many trades, and was it live or demo. Equity drawdown includes open positions, which is the number that reflects what you could actually withdraw. Balance drawdown ignores them, which is how a grid robot shows a calm curve until it collapses. A figure without a trade count is meaningless, because a short sample flatters almost any strategy. And a demo record is a description of a demo.

## Truth six: position size should be derived, never chosen

This is the truth that saves accounts, and it is the one free-EA pages skip. A gold scalper will have losing streaks; the size of each loss is what turns that streak into a survivable week or a closed account.

Derive the lot from the stop and a fixed risk percentage, never the other way round. If gold needs a 2.50-dollar stop on your timeframe and you are willing to risk 0.5 percent of a $1,000 account, the lot size solves to roughly 0.02 on a 100-ounce contract. That is the number, and it does not change because a bigger lot would make the returns look better. Choosing the lot first inverts the relationship, so your risk quietly grows exactly when the market is more volatile and the stop is wider.

The arithmetic of a losing streak is worth stating plainly. At 0.5 percent risk per trade, a run of five losses costs about 2.5 percent. At 2 percent, the same run costs about 10 percent. On a scalper, five losses in a row is an ordinary event, not a crisis. The strategy did not change between those two accounts; the size did, and the size decided the outcome. ## Truth seven: what the EA does when the market gaps

Gold gaps over weekends and jumps through stops during news, and a scalper's stop is not a promised price — it is an instruction that becomes a market order when touched. In fast conditions you can be filled well beyond the level you set, which means the risk you calculated is the risk you hoped for, not the risk you always get.

Three practical responses, none of them optional on gold. First, cap the total daily loss so one bad session cannot compound into a bad week. Second, stand aside inside the window around scheduled releases rather than trading through them. Third, keep the position small enough that a gap of several times the normal spread is survivable, because on gold that is a realistic single-event move, not a tail case. ESMA's product intervention measures cap retail leverage on gold at 20:1 and require negative balance protection, which limits the worst case but does not remove it. That is why the sizing decisions above matter more than any setting the EA ships with.

Add the EA version, preset name, broker server and symbol suffix at the top of the sheet. ## How do you stress-test a preset before it trades live?

A preset is a set of assumptions about the market. Stress-testing finds out which assumptions break first, and it belongs before the account is funded rather than after.

**Test the costs, not just the strategy.** Re-run the backtest that looked good with the spread, commission and swap your broker actually charges. A gold scalper can win the entries and still lose the account to costs, because a strategy targeting a few dollars per trade hands a fixed toll to the broker on every round trip.

**Change one assumption at a time.** Widen the spread by fifty percent and re-run. Add the slippage you measured on demo and re-run. Shift every entry by one bar and re-run. When performance collapses after a single change, you have found the dependency that matters, and it is usually spread or timing rather than the entry rule.

**Read the worst week, not the average month.** Sort the equity curve into weekly buckets and study the bottom five. That distribution describes what the account has to survive, and it is exactly what a monthly return conceals.

### The three failure modes worth naming before you install

- **The stop sits inside the noise.** Gold moves several dollars without changing anything. A stop tighter than that turns ordinary variation into a loss, and no exit rule repairs it.
- **The position is chosen rather than derived.** When the lot is fixed while the stop moves, risk varies silently with volatility, and it is largest exactly when the market is fastest.
- **The session is wrong.** Running a London-to-New-York scalper through the Asian range, or holding it through a policy announcement, is a different strategy from the one that was tested.

### Why one clean stress test beats ten optimised backtests

Optimisation is good at finding settings that fit the past, and it says nothing about whether those settings sit on a plateau or a spike. A stress test answers the more useful question: if the market is a little worse than the sample, does this survive? Two or three deliberate perturbations will describe the shape of the risk far better than another hundred parameter combinations describe the height of the curve.

Keep the results. Write the spread, the slippage, the dates and the version into a file beside the preset. When the live account diverges from the demo, that record is the only thing that lets you find out why — and if the preset survives the cost increase and the timing shift, run it on demo for a defined sample and then leave it alone.

### What the stress test cannot tell you

It cannot tell you what will happen, only what your assumptions have to survive. A preset that holds up under a wider spread and a shifted entry has demonstrated robustness, not a result. Keep that distinction, because it is the difference between evidence and reassurance.

Nor can it rescue a strategy whose logic you have not read. If the entry rule depends on something that exists only in the past — a repainting indicator, a value that looks ahead, or a signal that recalculates its last few bars — the numbers will look fine in every test and change their answer in live conditions. Reading the source is the only check for that, and it costs minutes rather than a testing cycle.

Finally, it says nothing about execution when volatility spikes. Stress-testing inside a backtest is a simulation of costs; the live version arrives as slippage on the entry you believed you had. Measure it on demo, compare it against what the backtest assumed, and adjust the preset before it runs on real money. Record the outcome either way, because the next scalper you evaluate will raise exactly the same three questions.
